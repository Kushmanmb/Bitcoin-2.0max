Bitcoin2.0Max + Ethereum

The Manifesto

Own your keys. Verify the system. Build something that lasts.

We believe people deserve tools that make digital ownership understandable, useful, and accessible.

Bitcoin2.0Max exists to explore that possibility through open-source infrastructure, practical applications, and a community willing to build in public.

Our ambition is large. Our progress must be measurable.

1. Respect the Foundations

Bitcoin demonstrated the power of peer-to-peer value transfer and independent verification. Ethereum expanded what people could build with programmable assets and smart contracts.

We draw inspiration from both traditions: the discipline to verify and the freedom to create.

Bitcoin2.0Max is an independent project. Its name does not make it an official Bitcoin upgrade, and displaying Ethereum data does not merge the two networks.

2. Put Ownership First

People should understand what they control, what they authorize, and which services they depend on.

Our direction is to support user-controlled wallets, clear permissions, and understandable transaction information.

An account on our website is a workspace for organizing information. Saving an address does not transfer ownership of that address or give the platform permission to spend its funds.

Private keys and recovery phrases do not belong in profiles, research notes, or community chat.

3. Make Verification a Habit

Trust should be earned through evidence.

We commit to distinguishing:

* Live network data from demonstrations.
* Confirmed transactions from pending activity.
* Working features from planned features.
* Independent validation from information supplied by third parties.

A dashboard that looks active is not proof of a working blockchain. Every claim should have an implementation, a test, or a clearly stated limitation behind it.

4. Build Useful Tools

Our vision is a connected workspace where people can:

* Follow Bitcoin and Ethereum network activity.
* Organize public addresses and transaction bookmarks.
* Track portfolios and maintain research notes.
* Set personal goals and follow their progress.
* Learn about node operation and blockchain infrastructure.
* Exchange ideas with other members.

These tools should make complex information easier to understand. Good design should help people make informed decisions.

5. Earn Performance

Faster blocks and greater capacity are engineering goals that come with tradeoffs.

We will evaluate changes against security, bandwidth, storage, reliability, and the ability of ordinary people to operate nodes.

Published targets are not proven results. Benchmarks, testing, and independent review must support performance claims.

6. Develop in the Open

Open source means inviting scrutiny.

We value readable code, documented decisions, reproducible builds, and contributions that improve the project’s foundations.

When something breaks, we should acknowledge it, explain its impact, and fix it. Honest limitations are more useful than impressive promises.

Security is ongoing work, never a badge that ends the conversation.

7. Connect Without Confusing

Supporting multiple networks should make their differences clearer.

Addresses, balances, fees, and transactions must identify the network they belong to. A combined interface does not make assets interchangeable.

Any future bridge, wrapped asset, or cross-chain feature must explain its trust assumptions and risks before people use it.

8. Build a Community Worth Joining

Questions belong here. Constructive criticism belongs here. Beginners belong here.

Our community should reward useful contributions, patient explanations, and respectful disagreement.

Impersonation, harassment, scams, and requests for recovery phrases undermine everything we are trying to build.

Technology creates the meeting place. People give it purpose.

9. Measure Progress Honestly

Bitcoin2.0Max is under active development.

Native blockchain operation, synchronization, consensus, and production readiness must be demonstrated before they are described as complete.

We make no promise of investment returns, token prices, or guaranteed rewards.

Our measure of success is working software, clearer information, and tools people can use with confidence.

The Invitation

Read the code. Ask difficult questions. Test a feature. Report a bug. Improve the documentation. Help another person understand what they are using.

We honor the foundations by taking the work seriously.

Build openly. Verify carefully. Keep moving forward.
